SLIDE 2 — Recap: Problem & Approach
==================================================
[ID] Sebelum masuk hasil, izinkan saya mengingatkan masalah dan pendekatannya.
WAF bekerja dengan mencocokkan pola serangan. Masalahnya, sebuah payload bisa
kita ubah BENTUK PERMUKAANNYA — misalnya menyisipkan komentar atau encoding —
tanpa mengubah makna serangannya. Saya memodelkan ini sebagai keputusan
bertahap: di tiap langkah, agen memilih satu mutasi. Agen baru dianggap
berhasil kalau payloadnya benar-benar lolos WAF DAN berhasil menarik data dari
database. Saya membandingkan tiga algoritma RL — PPO, TRPO, dan A2C — melawan
dua jenis WAF: ModSecurity yang berbasis aturan, dan Safeline yang berbasis
machine learning.

[EN] Before the results, let me recap the problem and approach. A WAF works by
matching attack patterns. The problem is that a payload's SURFACE FORM can be
changed — for example by inserting comments or encoding — without changing its
attack meaning. I model this as a step-by-step decision: at each step the agent
picks one mutation. The agent only counts as successful if the payload truly
passes the WAF AND actually extracts data from the database. I compare three RL
algorithms — PPO, TRPO, and A2C — against two kinds of WAF: rule-based
ModSecurity, and ML-based Safeline.

[EN] This research answers three questions. First, which RL algorithm is most

Third, whether the ORDER of mutations affects success. All three now have

SLIDE 4 — Metrics (IFNR & SPBARC)
==================================================
[ID] Supaya angka-angka nanti gampang dibaca, saya jelaskan dua metrik utamanya
dulu. Yang pertama, IFNR — Induced False Negative Rate. Intinya: seberapa SERING
agen berhasil membuat WAF kebobolan. Karena di setup saya WAF memblokir SEMUA
payload mentah, IFNR ini praktis sama dengan tingkat keberhasilan agen — makin
tinggi makin bagus. Yang kedua, SPBARC: rata-rata berapa kali percobaan yang
dibutuhkan untuk satu keberhasilan — ini ukuran EFISIENSI, jadi makin rendah
makin bagus. Agen ideal punya IFNR tinggi dan SPBARC rendah.

[EN] So the numbers later are easy to read, let me first explain the two main
metrics. The first is IFNR — Induced False Negative Rate. Essentially: how OFTEN
the agent manages to make the WAF fail to block. Because in my setup the WAF
blocks ALL raw payloads, IFNR is practically the agent's success rate — higher
is better. The second is SPBARC: the average number of attempts needed per
successful bypass — a measure of EFFICIENCY, so lower is better. An ideal agent
has high IFNR and low SPBARC.

SLIDE 5 — RQ1: Union corpus (ModSecurity)
==================================================
[ID] Ini hasil RQ1 di corpus union melawan ModSecurity. TRPO jelas paling
unggul, dengan IFNR 99 persen dan SPBARC paling kecil — artinya paling sering
berhasil sekaligus paling hemat. Diikuti PPO, lalu A2C. Yang menarik, ketiganya
sama-sama menguasai tier mudah dan medium; pembedanya ada di tier complex, yaitu
payload yang butuh rantai mutasi paling panjang. Ini mendukung hipotesis bahwa
metode dengan "rem" seperti trust-region dan clipping lebih baik daripada A2C
polos saat reward-nya jarang dan butuh banyak langkah.

[EN] These are the RQ1 results on the union corpus against ModSecurity. TRPO is
clearly the best, with 99 percent IFNR and the lowest SPBARC — meaning it
succeeds most often and most efficiently. Then PPO, then A2C. Interestingly, all
three master the easy and medium tiers; the difference appears in the complex
tier, the payloads that need the longest mutation chains. This supports the
hypothesis that methods with a "brake" like trust-region and clipping outperform
vanilla A2C when reward is sparse and many steps are needed.

SLIDE 6 — RQ1: Error corpus (NEW, multi-seed)
==================================================
[ID] Lalu saya menambahkan corpus baru: payload error-based, yang jauh lebih
sulit. Dan supaya adil, tiap algoritma saya latih TIGA KALI dengan seed acak
berbeda. Ada dua temuan. Pertama, soal ini memang jauh lebih sulit — IFNR
terbaik turun dari 99 ke sekitar 30 persen. Kedua, dan ini poin pentingnya: A2C
ternyata sangat TIDAK STABIL. Simpangannya sekitar 14 persen, kira-kira tujuh
kali lipat TRPO dan PPO — hasilnya bisa 6 persen, bisa 31 persen, hanya karena
seed acaknya berbeda. Justru karena saya menjalankan tiga seed inilah saya tahu:
ada satu run A2C yang nilainya 0. Kalau saya laporkan angka itu sendirian, saya
akan salah menyimpulkan A2C "kolaps total", padahal sebenarnya dia cuma labil.

[EN] Then I added a new corpus: error-based payloads, which are much harder. And
to be fair, I trained each algorithm THREE TIMES with different random seeds.
Two findings. First, this task is indeed much harder — the best IFNR drops from
99 to about 30 percent. Second, and this is the key point: A2C turns out to be
highly UNSTABLE. Its standard deviation is about 14 percent, roughly seven times
that of TRPO and PPO — its result can be 6 percent or 31 percent, just from a
different random seed. It's precisely because I ran three seeds that I know one
A2C run scored 0. If I had reported that single number, I would have wrongly
concluded A2C "completely collapses", when in reality it's just unstable.

SLIDE 7 — RQ3: Mutation Order Matters
==================================================
[ID] RQ3: apakah urutan mutasi penting. Jawabannya ya, sangat. Saya menemukan 35
pasangan mutasi yang konsisten di seluruh algoritma, di mana kalau urutannya
dibalik, tingkat keberhasilannya jatuh drastis. Contohnya, mutasi ident_backtick
harus dilakukan sebelum hex_to_char; kalau dibalik, keberhasilannya nol. Artinya
urutan ini bukan kebetulan dari satu algoritma, tapi sinyal STRUKTURAL — ia
mencerminkan bagaimana aturan-aturan WAF saling bergantung.

[EN] RQ3: does mutation order matter. The answer is yes, very much. I found 35
mutation pairs consistent across all algorithms where, if you reverse the order,
the success rate collapses. For example, the ident_backtick mutation must come
before hex_to_char; reversed, the success is zero. This means the ordering isn't
an accident of one algorithm, but a STRUCTURAL signal — it reflects how the WAF's
rules depend on each other.

SLIDE 8 — RQ2: Transfer ModSecurity -> Safeline
==================================================
[ID] Sekarang bagian utama: RQ2. Saya ambil model yang sudah dilatih di
ModSecurity, lalu langsung saya uji ke Safeline TANPA dilatih ulang — namanya
zero-shot transfer. Hasilnya: NOL persen, untuk semua algoritma dan kedua corpus.
Semua sekitar 900 payload termutasi diblokir. Ini bukan karena agennya menyerah;
ia benar-benar tidak bisa tembus. Tapi saya harus memastikan nol ini bukan karena
Safeline-nya memblokir segalanya. Maka saya jalankan positive control: request
normal lolos, SQLi standar diblokir, dan payload khusus yang saya susun manual
berhasil tembus dan menarik data. Jadi Safeline memang BISA ditembus — nol persen
itu murni karena strategi dari ModSecurity tidak cocok.

[EN] Now the main part: RQ2. I take the models trained on ModSecurity and test
them directly on Safeline WITHOUT retraining — this is zero-shot transfer. The
result: ZERO percent, for all algorithms and both corpora. All ~900 mutated
payloads were blocked. This isn't because the agent gave up; it genuinely cannot
get through. But I had to make sure this zero isn't because Safeline blocks
everything. So I ran a positive control: a normal request passes, a textbook
SQLi is blocked, and a special payload I crafted manually does get through and
extract data. So Safeline IS bypassable — the zero percent is purely because the
ModSecurity strategy doesn't fit.

SLIDE 9 — RQ2: Direct training (the key finding)
==================================================
[ID] Ini slide terpenting saya. Saya ringkas tiga situasi. Di ModSecurity yang
berbasis aturan, agen bisa lolos WAF SEKALIGUS menarik data — keberhasilan
tinggi. Di Safeline dengan transfer, tidak bisa apa-apa. Tapi yang menarik:
ketika saya latih agen LANGSUNG di Safeline, dia berhasil lolos WAF sekitar 89
persen — tapi menarik data NOL persen. Inilah temuan kuncinya: di WAF berbasis
aturan, "lolos" dan "mencuri data" itu satu paket; tapi di WAF berbasis machine
learning, keduanya TERPISAH. Buktinya, 846 dari 972 request statusnya 200 tapi
malah error SQL — artinya payloadnya lolos justru karena SQL-nya sudah rusak.

[EN] This is my most important slide. I summarize three situations. On rule-based
ModSecurity, the agent can pass the WAF AND extract data — high success. On
Safeline via transfer, it can do nothing. But here's the interesting part: when I
train the agent DIRECTLY on Safeline, it passes the WAF about 89 percent of the
time — but extracts data ZERO percent. This is the key finding: on rule-based
WAFs, "passing" and "stealing data" come together; but on ML-based WAFs, the two
SPLIT apart. The evidence: 846 of 972 requests returned status 200 but a SQL
error — meaning the payload passed precisely because the SQL was already broken.


==================================================
SLIDE 10 — RQ2: Why "passes but steals nothing"
==================================================
[ID] Kenapa bisa begitu? Karena detektor machine learning menilai MAKNA, bukan
pola permukaan. Supaya terlihat tidak berbahaya, agen akhirnya konvergen ke satu
rantai mutasi yang MERUSAK: misalnya double-encoding yang membuat kata UNION tak
lagi dikenali sebagai keyword, dan trik GBK yang merusak tanda kutip di backend.
Akibatnya, baik Safeline maupun database sama-sama tidak melihatnya sebagai SQL
yang valid — jadi lolos, tapi error, tidak ada data. Pelajarannya: di WAF
berbasis ML, agen hanya bisa menghindar dengan mengorbankan serangannya sendiri.

[EN] Why does this happen? Because the machine-learning detector judges MEANING,
not surface patterns. To look harmless, the agent ends up converging to one
DESTRUCTIVE mutation chain: for example double-encoding that makes the word UNION
no longer recognized as a keyword, and a GBK trick that breaks the quote on the
backend. As a result, neither Safeline nor the database sees it as valid SQL — so
it passes, but errors, with no data. The lesson: on ML-based WAFs, the agent can
only evade by sacrificing its own attack.

SLIDE 11 — Positioning & Novelty (vs BWAFSQLi)
==================================================
[ID] Saya ingin jujur soal posisi kontribusi ini. Ada karya bernama BWAFSQLi yang
sudah berhasil menembus Safeline dengan payload yang menjaga semantik, dan bahkan
sudah mengalahkan metode berbasis RL. Jadi klaim "RL menemukan bypass Safeline"
bukan kontribusi saya. Yang benar-benar baru dari riset saya ada tiga: pertama,
mengukur seberapa besar gap transfer dari WAF aturan ke WAF ML; kedua, analisis
kausal soal urutan mutasi; dan ketiga, penemuan bahwa "lolos" dan "mencuri data"
terpisah di WAF ML, lengkap dengan mekanismenya. Jadi posisi saya: saya
mempelajari MENGAPA strategi struktural bisa atau tidak bisa ditransfer — bukan
membuat alat bypass baru.

[EN] I want to be honest about how this contribution is positioned. There is a
work called BWAFSQLi that already bypasses Safeline with semantic-preserving
payloads, and even beats RL-based methods. So the claim "RL finds a Safeline
bypass" is not my contribution. What is genuinely new in my work is threefold:
first, measuring how large the transfer gap is from a rule-based to an ML-based
WAF; second, a causal analysis of mutation ordering; and third, the finding that
"passing" and "stealing data" decouple on ML-based WAFs, with its mechanism. So
my position is: I study WHY structural strategies do or don't transfer — not
building a new bypass tool.

[ID] Q: "Kenapa A2C tidak stabil?"  -> Karena A2C tak punya mekanisme 'rem'
(trust-region/clip) seperti TRPO/PPO; update-nya agresif, jadi titik start acak
(seed) sangat menentukan apakah ia belajar policy bagus atau nyangkut.
[EN] Q: "Why is A2C unstable?" -> A2C lacks a 'brake' (trust-region/clip) like
TRPO/PPO; its updates are aggressive, so the random starting point (seed) largely
decides whether it learns a good policy or gets stuck.

[ID] Q: "Kenapa transfer 0% itu menarik, bukan kegagalan?" -> Karena ia mengukur
sesuatu yang belum pernah diukur: seberapa besar strategi rule-based GAGAL pindah
ke WAF ML — plus kami buktikan target-nya memang bisa ditembus (positive control).
[EN] Q: "Why is the 0% transfer interesting, not a failure?" -> Because it
measures something not measured before: how badly a rule-based strategy fails to
move to an ML WAF — and we proved the target IS bypassable (positive control).
